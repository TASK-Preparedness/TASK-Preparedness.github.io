The list below follows the selected build. Checked items drop out of the remaining cost and the shopping list. Your checks stay in this browser only.

  • The development Pi is a Raspberry Pi 5 (8 GB). Volunteer testing for this alpha is requested only on a Pi 5 running Debian 13 “trixie” arm64 (or 64-bit Raspberry Pi OS based on trixie), on a spare SD card. Other memory sizes exist; 2 GB may be tight with SDR and the WebEngine reader, and they are untested. Public packages have not been validated on a physical Pi.

  • Raspberry Pi recommends a high-quality 5 V / 5 A USB-C supply, such as the official 27W PSU, so the board can pass enough current to USB peripherals. This accessory is not listed in HARDWARE.md as T.A.S.K.-tested; treat the official part as the manufacturer’s recommendation. UNVERIFIED as a T.A.S.K. item.

  • Raspberry Pi 5 performs best with active cooling. The official case already includes a fan, so you do not also need the stand-alone Active Cooler unless you are not using that case. Not listed in HARDWARE.md. UNVERIFIED as a T.A.S.K. item.

  • INSTALL.md asks for a spare SD card and about 300 MB free plus room for maps or ZIM files you add yourself. 128 GB is a comfortable size, not a hard requirement. Flash Debian 13 or Raspberry Pi OS 64-bit (trixie) yourself; do not assume a preloaded card is trixie or 64-bit. UNVERIFIED as a T.A.S.K. item.

  • The installer and launcher expect a desktop session. Any working HDMI display is enough; T.A.S.K. does not require a particular screen. This line is a typical desktop cost, not a T.A.S.K.-tested accessory. UNVERIFIED.

  • Needed for a desktop session. Not listed in HARDWARE.md. UNVERIFIED as a T.A.S.K. item.

  • HARDWARE.md lists a u-blox 7 USB GPS (via gpsd) as verified on the development system. gpsd owns the device; T.A.S.K. reads gpsd only. Other gpsd-supported receivers are untested. Public packages are still not validated on a physical Pi.

  • HARDWARE.md lists a Heltec WiFi LoRa 32 V3 (915 MHz, CP2102 USB) as verified for Meshtastic over USB serial on the development system. Use the frequency band that is legal where you are (US builders typically want 902–928 MHz). Heltec V3.2, T-Beam, T-Deck and other Meshtastic USB nodes are untested. A Meshtastic node transmits on LoRa when it sends or routes traffic — you must use legal settings. “Meshtastic” is a trademark of the Meshtastic project. Public packages are not Pi-validated.

  • HARDWARE.md lists RTL-SDR (RTL2832U-family) as untested with SDR Studio 0.2.4 — physical acceptance test pending — so this is UNVERIFIED. SDR Studio is receive-only. The RTL-SDR Blog V4 is untested specifically and needs a SoapySDR/librtlsdr build that supports the V4 tuner. Do not attach a transmit-capable SDR chain and expect this software to make it legal or safe.

  • Not listed in HARDWARE.md. UNVERIFIED. Raspberry Pi 5 is specified at 5 V / 5 A DC via USB-C with Power Delivery. Do not assume a laptop-style PD bank will keep the station stable under load until you have tested it yourself.

  • Not listed in HARDWARE.md. UNVERIFIED. Useful for a field bag; it is not required to run the software. This site does not recommend a particular brand.

  • A Pi 5 has two USB 3 and two USB 2 ports. A hub is only needed when you run out of ports or want to power several dongles more comfortably. Not listed in HARDWARE.md. UNVERIFIED.

Three recommended builds

Starter station

A basic desktop Pi 5 for learning, installing the experimental packages, and using the offline library once you add your own files. No radios. About $309–$450 if you buy every line, or much less if you already have a monitor, keyboard and card.

6 parts · core platform only

Communications station

Adds a USB GPS (u-blox 7 class), a Meshtastic USB node (Heltec V3 915 MHz class) and a receive-only RTL-SDR. This is the first build that exercises Maps & GPS, Mesh Network and SDR. About $372–$550.

9 parts · GPS, mesh, SDR

Portable field station

Adds portable power, a protective transport case and a USB hub. These extras are not in HARDWARE.md and are UNVERIFIED. About $452–$755, plus whatever you already counted in Comms.

12 parts · power, transport, USB

Starter totals $309–$450; Field totals $452–$755. The large figure above follows the tab you have selected (default: Comms).

Optional expansion: Radio / APRS

Not part of the three build totals. T.A.S.K. talks to an already running Direwolf instance through a soundcard TNC and a radio. Transmit stays off until a licensed operator enters their own callsign and enables it.

  • HARDWARE.md describes Radio / APRS as “Direwolf soundcard TNC + radio,” experimental, with the receive path in use and transmit only by explicit action. DigiRig and Mobilinkd are examples of that class of interface (audio and PTT, or a KISS TNC). They are not named in HARDWARE.md, so treat both as UNVERIFIED. A radio programming cable is not a TNC and will not give you APRS by itself. Amateur transmit in T.A.S.K. stays off until you enter your own licensed callsign; do not transmit if you are not licensed and allowed to. BTECH UV-PRO over Bluetooth is unverified and no support is claimed.

A programming cable for a handheld radio is not a TNC. GMRS data in this alpha uses a simulated driver only; do not assume a GMRS radio can legally be keyed by a soundcard modem.

Price sources and assumptions

  • Raspberry Pi 5 8 GB: $175 official list in the April 2026 Raspberry Pi 5 product brief; $175 at PiShop US (approved reseller), checked 26 November 2026. Raspberry Pi announced further memory-driven price rises in 2026.
  • 27W USB-C PSU: $12 official (Raspberry Pi, when introducing the $15 45W supply); $12.95 at PiShop US.
  • Official Pi 5 case: $10 (Raspberry Pi announcement and official magazine).
  • Official 128 GB A2 V30 microSD: $37.95 at PiShop US; the low end of the range is a typical generic A2/V30 card.
  • Display, keyboard, mouse, cable, hub, transport case and portable power: typical US street ranges for generic goods, not a specific listing.
  • Heltec V3: $17.90–$19.90 on heltec.org (checked against public November 2026 listings); about $25–$27 at US retailers.
  • USB u-blox 7 GPS: typical $10–$20 street range for generic “G-mouse” receivers.
  • RTL-SDR Blog V3 with dipole kit: $44.95 on rtl-sdr.com; dongle-only often about $35.
  • DigiRig Mobile: $54.95 on digirig.net; Mobilinkd TNC4: $149.95 on store.mobilinkd.com. Both are UNVERIFIED examples.

Assumptions: US retail, one of each line, no tax or shipping, no official T.A.S.K. kit (none is offered here), and a display if you do not already have one — that single line is most of the Starter range. Check off anything you already own.
